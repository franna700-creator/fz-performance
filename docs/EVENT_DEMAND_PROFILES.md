# FZ Performance Event Demand Profiles

Status: v0.7 FOUNDATION — prepared, not active coaching policy.

## Rule
An event name is not enough information for FZ intelligence. Every event family must have a **demand profile** describing what the athlete actually has to do: sequence, running dose, station dose, external load, transition density and event-specific skills.

Cross-event transfer is then calculated at the demand/capability level. Shared demands may contribute evidence to the primary objective, but no result is treated as interchangeable with another event.

## Deadly Dozen Track · Male Singles · South Africa
Official South African format: 12 x 400 m Journeys, each followed by one Labour, for 4.8 km total running and 12 stations. Current male loads are retained in the format registry.

The sequence is:
1. 400 m + 240 m Farmers Carry · 2 x 24 kg kettlebells
2. 400 m + 60 Deadlifts · 1 x 32 kg kettlebell
3. 400 m + Lunge · 2 x 12.5 kg dumbbells
4. 400 m + 60 Snatches · 1 x 15 kg dumbbell
5. 400 m + 60 m Burpee Broad Jump
6. 400 m + 60 Goblet Squats · 1 x 16 kg kettlebell
7. 400 m + 240 m Front Carry · 25 kg plate
8. 400 m + 60 Push Press · 2 x 12.5 kg dumbbells
9. 400 m + 120 m Bear Crawl
10. 400 m + 60 Clean & Press · 15 kg plate
11. 400 m + 180 m Overhead Carry · 15 kg plate
12. 400 m + 20 Devil Press · 2 x 10 kg dumbbells

Note: the current South African web page displays the lunge as `60x Lunge`; the global Track standard describes 60 m. FZ retains this source discrepancy rather than silently normalising it. The event-specific rulebook/race brief should become authoritative when available.

## HYROX · Singles Open Men
Official current format: 8 x 1 km runs, each followed by one station, for 8 km running plus:
1. 1000 m SkiErg
2. 50 m Sled Push · 152 kg including sled
3. 50 m Sled Pull · 103 kg including sled
4. 80 m Burpee Broad Jump
5. 1000 m Row
6. 200 m Farmers Carry · 2 x 24 kg
7. 100 m Sandbag Lunges · 20 kg
8. 100 Wall Balls · 6 kg

## Transfer into HYROX
### Near-direct
- Farmers Carry: same 2 x 24 kg male load. Deadly Dozen is longer at 240 m vs 200 m.
- Burpee Broad Jump: same movement; 60 m vs 80 m.

### Strong
- Loaded lunges: same unilateral locomotor demand, different load placement/distance.
- Compromised running: both repeatedly ask the athlete to run after functional work. Deadly Dozen has 12 shorter 400 m bouts and higher transition frequency; HYROX requires 8 longer 1 km bouts and 8 km total running.

### Moderate
- Goblet squat, push press, clean & press and devil press contribute squat/overhead and shoulder endurance useful for wall balls, but they do not replace wall-ball-specific rhythm, target accuracy and high-rep execution.
- Deadlifts/snatches/clean & press contribute posterior-chain and grip endurance useful around sled/row demands, but transfer is limited by different mechanics.

### HYROX capability not validated by Deadly Dozen
- SkiErg efficiency/pacing
- RowErg efficiency/pacing
- sled push force/mechanics
- sled pull force/rope handling
- 100-rep wall-ball execution
- maintaining useful pace across 1 km compromised segments and 8 km total running

## Half-marathon transfer
A 21.0975 km road half marathon has no station overlap. Its value to HYROX is foundational: running economy, aerobic durability, threshold/high-aerobic capacity, pacing discipline and running tissue durability. It does not validate transition recovery, station strength-endurance or compromised running directly.

## Source conflict rule
The athlete-entered event schedule and official event sources are separate evidence. An external Deadly Dozen source showed 19 Sep 2026, while Francois had scheduled the UJ event for Sunday 20 Sep 2026. Francois explicitly confirmed on 10 Sep 2026 that **20 Sep is correct for his race**. FZ therefore uses 20 Sep for runway/taper calculations and retains the external discrepancy only as provenance (`RESOLVED_ATHLETE_CONFIRMED`).

## Generic engine vs calibration anchors
The explicit Deadly Dozen/HYROX and half-marathon/HYROX transfer rules are **calibration anchors** for relationships we already know precisely. They are not a requirement for every future event. Once a future event has a qualified structure, FZ can derive overlap through the common demand fingerprint (running structure, modalities, movements/stations, transitions and capabilities). Exact known mappings can then calibrate that generic result when available.
